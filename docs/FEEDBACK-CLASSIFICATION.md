## The rule behind most of the DON'Ts

A visual language has a budget. Four encodings is roughly the limit of what a
person reads at once and we are already at four. Every addition costs
comprehension of the ones already there.

Each of drones, holograms, robots, statues, stadiums, storms and pollution
adds a little on its own. Together they turn an instrument into a toy, and
once it looks like a toy nobody argues with the numbers. Nobody arguing with
the numbers sounds like success and is the opposite: it means nobody is acting
on them either.

**This is Hilmi's own principle**, from his first note: keep the mechanics very
simple and make the visualisation the hero. Worth quoting back to him, because
it is the strongest argument against his own list and it is his.

## The points economy, if it goes ahead

Not a version of this. A different product, and the order is not what people
expect.

**1. Governance first, starting now.** Scoring individual behaviour and
displaying it comparatively is a works council matter in Germany and several
other markets, and a data protection question everywhere. Longest lead time of
anything here. It must start before the first point is scored, not after the
first screenshot circulates.

**2. Instrumentation second.** Every point needs an event and every event needs
a system to emit it: AVA, Ariba, SAC, the training platform, the marketplace.
Five integrations with five owners. **This is the real cost of the idea and it
is invisible in the description.**

**3. Rules third, with an appeals process.** Hilmi identifies the hard question
as which behaviours to reward. The harder one is underneath it: 500 points for
"measurable business value" against 10 for training means somebody adjudicates
the 500. Every points system becomes an argument about the adjudication, and
the argument arrives at whoever runs the board.

**4. Visuals last.** The city is a week of work on top of a quarter of plumbing.

**And one design note to carry in from the start.** His two notes answer each
other. Points accumulate and never decay, so a team that did everything in Q1
and nothing since keeps its skyscraper forever. His own sleeping-builders idea
is the fix: **height from what you have built, activity from what you are doing
now.** Stock and flow, both visible. Better than either note alone, and it is
his idea improved rather than trimmed.
