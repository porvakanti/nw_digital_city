# What we are building next

The concrete list, for sharing. Ten changes, in the order we would do them.
Everything here uses data we already hold, so none of it waits on anybody.

The separate list of what we need from other people is in the workbook, on the
"What we need" sheet, with a sample of the exact shape on the sheet after it.

---

## The four that change the argument

### 1. Show the real AI figures instead of the placeholder

The AI-generated RFP column was empty when the first version was built, so the
rooftop lights ran on stand-in data, badged on screen as sample. Tomas's new
extract has real numbers.

**Today the city shows 28 categories with AI activity. The truth is 8.** Only
four of them are the same ones.

*What changes:* the lit rooftops drop from 28 to 8, and the "sample data"
warning comes off, because the number is now real.

*Why it goes first:* this is a correction, not an improvement. Eight lit roofs
is a weaker picture than twenty-eight and a much better position to argue
from, because it is true.

### 2. Bring in the new "blueprint used" column

Tomas's refresh added a column nobody has seen on screen yet: whether each
blueprint has ever actually been used.

**44 categories have an active blueprint. Four have one that has ever been
used.**

*What changes:* every category card shows it, and the whole-city summary
carries it alongside the count of blueprints.

*Why:* it moves us from a coverage story to an adoption story, which is
harder, more useful and much more likely to change behaviour.

### 3. The empty building

A new state on the map: a finished building with its lights off and nothing
moving at the door. Forty of the forty-four built lots become empty buildings.

*What you see:* a skyline that is built and dark.

*What it lets us say:* "We have written 44 blueprints. Four of them have ever
been used. This is what that looks like."

*Why:* the strongest fact we have is currently impossible to see. This is the
change that earns the whole exercise.

### 4. Traditional, Connected, Smart, Autonomous as the spine

Hilmi's own arc, adopted as the organising story rather than four unrelated
measures. It needs no new data because the measures already on screen are that
arc:

| Stage | What it means in the data |
| --- | --- |
| **Traditional** | The blueprint exists and works in one market |
| **Connected** | One blueprint working across several markets |
| **Smart** | AI-generated RFPs have started |
| **Autonomous** | Above the top of the scale. Nobody is there yet |

*What changes:* the legend, the order of the guided tour, and a progress
figure in the top bar.

*Why:* it ranks nobody, it needs nothing new, and showing Autonomous as empty
is more honest than filling it in.

## The journey score

### 5. A single score, and its three parts

One number for a category, a district or all of Networks, weighted **40% for
how far the blueprint has got, 35% for anyone actually using it, and 25% for
doing it with AI**.

**Networks scores 19 out of 100.** The split is the point:

| | Score | Out of | In plain words |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blueprint | 16 | 40 | 40% of the way through writing them |
| Usage | **2** | **35** | **5% of the way through using them** |
| AI | 2 | 25 | 7% |

One category reaches 100: **A251 Network Professional Services**, active in
eight markets, used twice, two AI-generated RFPs. It is the only category that
has made the entire journey.

### 6. The score by district, on screen

Every district gets its score and the three parts behind it.

*Why by district:* it needs no minimum, it names nobody, and it is defensible
in public. **Five of the eight districts score zero on usage**, which is the
finding that should be visible.

### 7. Name the pioneers

Nine categories have reached the top stage. Mark them on the map and list them
by name.

*Why not a ranking:* on the measure that matters most, 31 of 35 category
managers are at zero, largely because of which categories they were handed. A
top five would put thirty named colleagues at zero on a screen. Nobody is
bottom of a list of pioneers, and the celebration does the motivational work
without the damage.

## The three visual changes from Hilmi's note

### 8. Remove the green land and size each plot by spend

Agreed in full. The parkland is dead space and plot size currently shows how
many categories are in a group, which is the least interesting thing about it.

**One adjustment.** Eighty-nine of the 145 categories have no recorded spend.
If individual lot size followed spend, those 89 would shrink to nothing and
the strongest finding we have would vanish off the screen. So the **plot** is
sized by spend and the **lots inside it stay even**. Area then means money and
the empty ones stay countable.

*What you see:* Managed Services and Outsourcing correctly becomes the biggest
thing on the map, carrying 37.7% of Networks spend from just 10 categories.

### 9. Everything that moves has to mean something

Adopted as a standing rule, not a one-off fix. Traffic density in each district
becomes that district's share of spend. Anything that cannot be justified gets
deleted rather than kept for decoration.

### 10. Landmarks for the most reused blueprints

Three categories are live in seven or more markets, and A221 Spring 2/R is
live in sixteen, more than double the next one. They get a landmark building.

This is one of Hilmi's copilot's ideas that we can do immediately, because the
data is already there.

## What we are deliberately not building

So it does not get raised again every fortnight. Full reasoning against each
item is in the workbook.

**Drones, holograms, robots, statues, stadiums, world wonders.** Four things
you can read at once is roughly the human limit and we are already at four.
Each of these adds a little alone and collectively turns an instrument into a
toy. Once it looks like a toy nobody argues with the numbers, which sounds
like success and is the opposite: it means nobody acts on them either.

This is Hilmi's own principle from his first note, that the mechanics should
stay simple and the visualisation should be the hero. We are taking him at his
word.

**Digital pollution, traffic jams and natural disasters.** A "Manual Process
Storm" over a named district is a public criticism of whoever owns it, on a
screen their director is watching. That works in a game people choose to play.
It does not work here.

**Roads and bridges for collaboration.** No collaboration data exists, and the
tempting proxy of shared markets drawn across 145 categories and 21 markets
produces something unreadable.

**Widening beyond Networks, for now.** The city is not Networks-specific: it
draws whatever category tree it is given, and scope is a single filter. So
widening is cheap whenever we want it. But 145 lots is already at the edge of
what reads from the back of a room, and this all-hands is a Networks
all-hands. Build it to widen, do not widen it for the day.
