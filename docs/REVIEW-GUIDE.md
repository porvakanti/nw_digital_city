# NW Digital City: how to look at it

A page to read before you open it, and a short list of things to try. Ten
minutes end to end. No installing anything, no network needed, nothing to log
in to.

## Opening it

Save the attached **NW Digital City.html** somewhere you can find it, then
double-click it. It opens in your browser like a web page.

Chrome or Edge. Both are fine. If it opens in something else and looks wrong,
right-click the file, Open with, Chrome.

It is one file, so there is nothing to unzip and no folder to hunt through.
The whole city is inside it: the data, the 3D, and the part that answers
questions. It works with the wifi off.

**Please use a laptop for this.** Not a preference: an iPhone or iPad cannot
open the attachment at all. iOS does not let Safari or Chrome open a file
saved on the device, and the preview in the Files app does not run the code
the city is built from, so you get a blank page. Nothing is wrong with the
file. Android is more forgiving but still awkward.

The city itself works perfectly well on a phone screen, with buttons along the
bottom instead of the keyboard shortcuts. It is only getting the file into the
browser that iOS refuses. When there is a proper web address for this, a phone
will be fine, and I will send it round.

## The first two minutes

The city builds itself when it loads. Let it finish.

Then press **T** for a guided tour. Nine steps, it drives itself, and you move
on with the space bar or by clicking Next. That is the fastest way in and it
shows you every part of it in order. Take the tour before you start poking at
things.

## What you are looking at

The whole of Networks procurement as a city. Eight districts, one per category
area. Inside each district are plots, and inside those, one lot per category.
145 lots, because there are 145 categories.

Four things about a category are visible at once:

| On screen | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| A foundation, or bare ground | Whether a category blueprint exists at all |
| How tall the building is | How many local markets have taken it up |
| Green houses, or a red hotel | Spend, in Monopoly bands |
| A light on the roof | Whether AI-generated RFPs have started there |

Bare ground is a category with no blueprint. There are 89 of them, and they
are meant to be the thing you notice.

`READING-THE-CITY.md` goes through all four in detail, with the thresholds and
what the numbers actually come to. You do not need it to look at the city, but
it is there if you want to argue with a figure.

## Six things to try

Type these into the box at the bottom of the screen and press enter.

1. **`batteries`** or **`A221`**. One category. Watch the panel on the right:
   it shows the agent finding it, looking up its numbers, and then building
   it. The building is torn down and reassembled in front of you.
2. **`Where are the biggest gaps?`** Flies to the most valuable piece of
   empty ground in Networks.
3. **`What could we build?`** The light drops, everything already built goes
   grey, and every unbuilt lot rises in cyan. This is the picture of the
   estate if every blueprint existed.
4. **`How is Energy doing?`** A whole district rather than one category.
5. **`Show me AI readiness`**. Night falls and the lit rooftops are the
   categories where AI-generated RFPs have started.
6. **`banana bread`**. Included on purpose. It says it cannot find that,
   rather than inventing something. It should never guess.

Also worth doing: **hover** any building to see what it is, and **click** one
to open its title deed, which is the Monopoly card for that category.

Keys, if you prefer them: **R** resets, **N** is night, **P** is what we could
build, **K** is the closing ask, **T** starts the tour again.

On a touchscreen, once there is a web address to open, those five are buttons
along the bottom instead: Tour, Reset, Night, Could be, The ask. Drag one
finger to move, pinch to zoom, and tap a building rather than hovering it.

## Two calls I have made that I would like you to overturn or confirm

**Height is market take-up, not adoption.** The adoption column in the extract
reads 100% for all 145 categories. If I used it, every building would be the
same maximum height and the skyline would say nothing. So height is currently
how many local markets have adopted the blueprint, which is real data, spreads
from 1 to 16, and tells a truer story. It is labelled provisional on screen
and swaps back in one line if the real adoption figures arrive. **Is that flat
100% real, or a placeholder in the source?**

**The spend bands are rescaled.** The narrative says the thresholds are still
to be decided. On the bands in the deck, under 5m euro through over 100m,
Networks would have no hotels at all and almost nothing above one house. I
have used under 1m through over 50m, which produces six hotels and a readable
spread. **These are yours to set. Tell me the numbers and it is a one line
change.**

## What would be most useful to hear back

Anything at all, but these especially:

1. Does the city read correctly to you, or does something look bigger,
   smaller or more built than it actually is?
2. Is there a question you would expect to be able to ask that it cannot
   answer? Please send the exact words you typed.
3. Which categories are likely to get called out on the day, so I can check
   they land well?
4. Are we comfortable showing that 89 of the 145 lots are empty ground? I
   think it is the strongest thing in it, but it is not my call.
5. Anything in the wording on screen that is off, in tone or in fact.

## Things that are known and do not need reporting

- **The AI readiness lights are placeholder data.** There is no real
  AI-generated RFP data yet. Labelled as such on screen.
- **Some categories show zero spend.** That is what the extract says. Whether
  it is a data gap or genuinely nothing is one of my open questions.
- **The question box uses simple rules in this version.** The full version
  runs a language model behind it, which handles much looser phrasing. What
  you have is the offline fallback, and it covers everything in this guide.
- **The attachment will not open on an iPhone or iPad.** That is iOS refusing
  to open a saved file in a browser, not a fault in the file. A laptop, for
  now.

## On the data

Everything comes from the category blueprint extract. Nothing is invented, and
where I have had to make a call it is flagged on screen rather than buried.

The source workbook has blueprint owner names and email addresses in it. What
you have is a derived extract with no names and no contact details in it at
all, and the build refuses to produce a file if it finds either.
